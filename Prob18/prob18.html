Patterns · Factors and squares

Sixteen sleepy doors.

Sixteen numbered doors start closed. In round k, toggle each door numbered with a multiple of k. Which doors are open at the end?

Round by round

A toggle flips a door: closed becomes open; open becomes closed. Each numbered door is selected here for a factor investigation; the round buttons follow the full rule.

Solid panel = closed · swung panel and star = open · gold outline = toggled in this round.
At round 0, all doors are closed.

Door 12 changes at rounds 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Door 9 changes at 1, 3, 9.

Its divisor pairs

Why squares stay open

Door d toggles once per positive divisor of d. Most factors pair up: a×b=d. Each pair gives two toggles, returning the door to closed. A square has one unpaired middle factor: for 9, 3×3. That leaves an odd toggle count, so it stays open.

Door 1 has one factor, 1, which is its unpaired middle factor. At the end, the open doors are exactly the square numbers: 1, 4, 9, 16. With N doors, there are ⌊√N⌋ open doors.

With 25 doors, the open labels are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25: five doors. Each is a perfect square, with one unpaired middle factor.
