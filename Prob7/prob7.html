24 squares. How many breaks?

A chocolate bar has 4 rows and 6 columns. Break it into 24 individual squares using as few breaks as possible.

The other pieces stay as they are. So every allowed move adds exactly one piece, wherever you break.

Pieces = 1 + breaks

Starting with 1 piece, after b breaks there are b + 1 pieces.

Optional: check the 24-square count
3 Why 23 breaks is the answer

Every break adds one piece. After 22 breaks there are only 23 pieces. After 23 breaks there are 24. That’s why every complete legal strategy needs exactly 23 breaks.

Optional: build a proof that fewer cannot work

Turn the discovery into a rule.

An r-row, c-column bar has r × c squares. To get that many separate pieces, you need:

Breaks = r × c − 1

Every complete legal strategy takes that many breaks. If a piece has more than one square, it can still be split, so the count is achievable.

What if stacking were allowed?

Then one move could break several pieces at once. “Every break adds exactly one piece” would no longer be true. Our proof depends on breaking only one piece per move.
