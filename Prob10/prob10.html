The little challenge

Can you guarantee a pair?

There are red, blue, and green socks mixed together in a dark drawer. Watch a worst-case example: the first three are all different; the fourth must match one. You cannot see a sock until you pull it out. What is the smallest number of socks you must take to guarantee two of the same color?

Why does this work?
In the worst case, your first three socks are one red, one blue, and one green. That still gives no pair. The fourth sock must match one of those three colors, so four guarantees a pair. This is the pigeonhole principle: four objects placed in three color groups force one group to contain at least two.
