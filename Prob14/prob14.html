The tower that moves.

Move the whole tower from A to C. What is the fewest number of moves?

The rules

Move one top disk at a time. A larger disk can never sit on a smaller one.

A is the start, B is the helper, C is the goal. The disks stay stacked around their peg.

Disk 1 is smallest; disk 4 is largest. Each disk is numbered and its width shows its size.
Hint: clear the largest disk
Before the largest disk can move, the smaller tower must be together on the other peg.

Choose a top disk.

The idea

To move n disks, first move the top n−1 disks aside, move the largest once, then rebuild the smaller tower: M(n)=2M(n−1)+1.

For three disks: 3+1+3=7. The table gives 0,1,3,7,15 moves for n=0…4. Since M(n)+1 doubles each time, M(n)=2n−1.

This is a lower bound as well as a strategy: before the largest disk's last arrival on C, the smaller tower needs at least M(n−1) moves; it also needs at least M(n−1) moves to finish above it. The largest disk needs at least one move. The strategy meets that bound, so it is minimal.
