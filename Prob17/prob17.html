The parity idea

At a middle visit to an island, each arrival pairs with a departure. Only the start and finish can have an unpaired bridge, so at most two islands can have odd degree. Four odd islands make the original connected map impossible.

Closing A–D changes parity at just A and D. B and C remain odd; start at one and finish at the other. For a connected map, a full route exists exactly when there are zero or two odd islands. Parity alone is not enough if a map is disconnected.

Why zero or two odd islands are enough

With zero odd islands, follow unused edges until returning to the start; if bridges remain, insert another closed walk where it meets the route. Connectedness lets these walks join. With two odd islands, imagine adding a bridge between them, make the closed walk, then remove that imagined bridge.

Transfer: a connected map has degrees A=2, B=3, C=3, D=4. A route is possible: start and finish at the two degree-3 islands, B and C.
