The grown-up mathematics

The number of bridges touching an island is its degree. An odd degree leaves one bridge unpaired. A walk that uses every bridge exactly once is an Euler trail.

For a connected map, such a walk exists exactly when it has zero or two odd-degree islands. With two, those islands must be its start and finish. With zero, it can return to its starting island. These maps are connected; separate groups of bridges would need a separate check.

Why is the condition sufficient? With all degrees even, keep following unused bridges to form a closed walk. If bridges remain, make another closed walk from a visited island and join it into the first. Connectedness makes this possible. With two odd islands, temporarily connect them, build a closed walk, then remove that extra bridge.
