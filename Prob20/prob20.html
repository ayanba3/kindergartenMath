Number theory · Euclid’s algorithm

The biggest square tile.

A 21 by 15 floor needs whole square tiles, with no cutting. What is the largest tile?

Same floor, smaller pieces

Any square tile that covers the 21 by 15 floor exactly must have a whole-number side length dividing both dimensions.

Teal grid lines show equal final tiles. Orange hatching marks leftover strips when a proposed size does not fit.

Why the possible sizes stay the same

A side s divides 21 and 15 exactly iff it divides 15 and the leftover 6 = 21 − 15. Likewise, s divides 15 and 6 iff it divides 6 and the leftover 3 = 15 − 2×6. Conversely, divisors of 15 and 6 also divide 21 = 15 + 6, and divisors of 6 and 3 divide 15 = 2×6 + 3. The possible sizes are unchanged at each cut.

So every common tile size must divide the last positive remainder, 3. Since 3 divides both original sides, it is the largest possible side.
