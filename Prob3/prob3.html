Discover the rule

Think about the very last hop.

Build the counts one stone at a time. Then turn your discovery into a formula.

1Start with a tiny journey
Try a prediction. It’s okay to change your mind.
3 Say the rule, then write it

Every route finishes with +1 or +2. These two groups include every route, and no route can have both last hops.

Ways to a stone = ways to the previous stone + ways to the stone two places back.

W(n) = W(n − 1) + W(n − 2)

W(n) means “the number of routes to stone n.” This rule works for n ≥ 2.

The starting counts: W(0) = 1 (one starting route: stay on the bank), and W(1) = 1 (one hop).

How can smaller journeys help?

To reach stone 6, Pip must make a last hop from stone 5 (+1) or stone 4 (+2). These are separate groups of routes, so we add them.

Choose a stone below to see where its routes come from.

There are 8 routes to stone 5 and 5 routes to stone 4. So there are 8 + 5 = 13 routes to stone 6.
