Order matters.

Three friends take three seats. Does doing the same two moves in a different order give the same result?

Try both orders

Three friends start in seats 1, 2, 3: Ari (A), Bo (B), Cam (C). R moves everyone one seat right, with seat 3 wrapping to seat 1. S swaps whoever currently sits in seats 1 and 2.

Seats stay fixed and numbered. Names and letters travel with each friend.

Tap R or S, then compare the fresh examples below.

Why the order matters

Starting at ABC, R then S ends at ACB, whose seat 1 has A. S then R ends at CBA, whose seat 1 has C. Since the result differs, the same two actions in opposite orders are different transformations.

Why there are six

There are 3 choices for seat 1, then 2 for seat 2, then 1 for seat 3: 3×2×1=6. R repeated three times returns every friend to the start; S repeated twice also returns everyone. So R can be undone by two R moves, while S undoes itself.

Optional formal detail: S₃

The six rearrangements with composition form the symmetric group S₃. The identity action leaves every friend in place. Every rearrangement has an inverse, and composition is associative: regrouping a sequence does not change its order or result. Associativity does not let us swap actions. R and S together generate all six arrangements.
